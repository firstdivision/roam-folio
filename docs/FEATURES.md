# Feature Requirements

Legend: **MVP** = first playable release; **Later** = planned enhancement; **Open** = decision needed.

## MVP — Daily guessing
- **MVP:** Every player receives the **same target country for the same local calendar date**, regardless of device or session. Different time zones unlock that date at different moments.
- **MVP:** A new puzzle begins at midnight in the browser's local time zone; show a countdown to that midnight after completion, accounting for daylight saving changes.
- **MVP:** Select the day's target deterministically from a curated eligible-country list; never pick independently for each visitor.
- **MVP:** Avoid repeating a target until the current eligible-country cycle is exhausted; use a stable, versioned schedule or deterministic permutation.
- **MVP:** Players guess by typing a country name through an accessible search interface with suggestions. Map clicks do not select or submit guesses; the map only displays countries and the guesses made.
- **MVP:** After a valid guess on touch devices, dismiss the software keyboard to reveal the map. Desktop submission keeps the country input focused for the next guess.
- **MVP:** Keep the country field and submit button above the fold on desktop and mobile, with a compact header and no large introductory banner. Place the entry above the map canvas, with no “Where shall we go?” heading, map heading, or zoom toolbar above the entry and no country-count/tagline line beneath the input. The input retains the accessible name “Country name” and a visible typing placeholder. After a guess, a compact result notice appears between the submit row and map.
- **MVP:** The display map supports drag/touch panning, wheel/pinch zoom up to 32×, and keyboard navigation including zoom/reset shortcuts. The map has no visible heading, zoom buttons, zoom percentage, or reset button. Navigating the map never selects or submits a guess.
- **MVP:** Each new distinct guess immediately refocuses the map without camera animation, aiming to frame its mapped country components and a conservative surrounding area based on the unrounded nearest-border distance, with a minimum 250-mile context radius and 10% viewport margins where possible. Automatic framing and all manual zoom controls have a fixed minimum scale of 1.2×, equal to the initial view. Large areas, antimeridian crossings, polar context, and dispersed geography may remain cropped at that scale; never shrink the map to an uncropped world overview. This camera uses only the guessed country and distance, never the hidden target's location, direction, or size. Complete target visibility is best effort, not guaranteed. Restore the latest chronological saved guess's framing immediately on reload. Duplicates and invalid submissions do not reframe. Manual navigation remains under the player's control until another distinct guess. Resize refits automatic views without replacing manual zoom choices; reset returns to the centered 1.2× start. A fresh local-date puzzle resets the camera. Map detail loading does not shift framing. Automatic framing does not scroll the page or move input focus.
- **MVP:** Country shapes have no hover-name tooltips, including previously guessed countries. Guess names and results remain available in the travel log.
- **MVP:** Load the overview map initially and fetch more detailed boundaries on zoom. If detail is unavailable, keep the overview usable and offer a retry.
- **MVP:** Allow unlimited guesses until the target is found. There is no give-up option or alternative discovery unlock.
- **MVP:** After every guess, display **numeric nearest-border distance to the target** (default miles; optional kilometers) and color the guessed country according to proximity.
- **MVP:** Feedback should be consistent for every player and guess. Use minimum distance between country boundaries; shared land borders display 0 miles and an explicit bordering message (see architecture).
- **MVP:** Show compact latest-guess feedback directly under the submit row in a highlighted single-line notice, without a heading: country, distance in `mi`, optional “Borders,” and optional “Closer,” “Farther,” or “Tied.” Omit redundant proximity words and “away” from the visible notice. Use 14 px text on desktop and 12 px on mobile; truncate only the country name with an ellipsis as needed, retaining the full accessible name and full name in history. From the second distinct guess, compare with the best earlier unrounded distance; first guesses have no comparison. Repeated guesses show their existing distance, compare against the current best, and add “Repeat” without increasing the count. Keep full descriptive feedback in the screen-reader live announcement. Restore the latest saved guess and comparison after reload; completion replaces the notice with a success modal and a “View your postcard” button below the map. Track guess count and history; mark guesses on the map; clearly indicate when the target is found. Sort history by unrounded distance, closest first. Show a correct guess first among zero-distance results; retain guess order for other ties and keep original guess numbers. Persistence and share emojis retain chronological guess order.
- **MVP:** Repeating a guessed country returns its existing result without increasing the guess count.
- **MVP:** Keep guess history compact: no visible “Your travel log” heading or guess-count badge. Retain an accessible history-region name, the “Closest guesses first” note, and each row's original guess number and results.
- **MVP:** Proximity uses a continuous gradient from white (very far away), through pale yellow, yellow and orange, to red (very close). The approved bands are 0–250 miles (Very close), over 250–1,000 (Close), over 1,000–3,000 (Far), over 3,000–6,000 (Farther away), over 6,000–9,000 (Distant), and over 9,000 (Very far away). The gradient reaches white at 9,000 miles. Map and history share a fixed global distance-to-color scale; labels and share emojis use the same distance bands, with orange and yellow emojis reused for their intermediate shades. Labels and numeric distances accompany color; outlines distinguish white guesses from unguessed countries.
- **MVP:** Preserve current day's guesses and completion status across reloads on the same device, without an account.
- **MVP:** Track average guesses per solve across completed puzzles saved in this browser, including earlier saved completions and today's solve. Count each puzzle once; exclude unfinished puzzles and repeated guesses. Show the average to one decimal place on the postcard, with the number of solved puzzles, and include it in both native and copyable share text. Clearing browser data clears this history. If storage is unavailable, the current solve remains displayable and shareable.
- **MVP:** Do not reveal the destination's name, facts, or food before the player solves the puzzle.
- **MVP:** After solving, automatically open an accessible modal dialog containing “Destination discovered,” the destination name, guess count, sharing, local-midnight countdown, and a postcard with three sourced facts, one cultural note, and at least one signature food. The dialog opens for restored completed puzzles too. Include a visible close button, Escape dismissal, focus containment, background scroll locking, and focus restoration to a “View your postcard” button that reopens it. Keep long postcard content scrollable on small screens, with the close button reachable. No recipes are required for MVP.
- **MVP:** Each of the 146 answer-country postcards includes a freely reusable, locally stored photograph directly below “A few things to take with you” and above its facts. Render and fetch it only after solving. Include descriptive alternative text, a source link, photographer attribution, the individual license with a link, and any resize notice. Preserve the full image aspect ratio on phones and desktop. Photo failure leaves the facts, credits, and completion controls usable. Track file-level provenance and checksums; guess-only countries do not need photos.
- **MVP:** Responsive and accessible on mobile and desktop. Start the map centered at 1.2× zoom, with reset returning to that view. Use a compact desktop canvas (1000:430 aspect ratio, at most 410 px tall) and a 300–400 px mobile viewport (48% of the small viewport height); fill it and allow panning across the cropped world. Remove the bottom “Farther / Closer” legend and visible map instructions; retain screen-reader instructions and guidance in “How to play.”
- **MVP:** Once solved, offer spoiler-free text sharing with `Roam-folio: <date>`, `Today: <guess count>`, and `Average: <average guesses>` on separate lines, then a blank line and the ordered proximity emojis: white square for very far away, yellow and orange squares for intermediate distances, red square for very close, and a distinct success marker. The average uses one decimal place. Do not include a URL, country names, or discovery content. Provide a copyable text fallback when native sharing is unavailable.
- **MVP:** Shared website links include static, spoiler-free Open Graph metadata and a branded preview image readable without JavaScript.

## Country pool
- **MVP:** Use the explicitly approved 146-country answer pool and 197-country guess registry. The initial 125 answers and subsequent Chad addition are retained; on 2026-10-25 the user approved 20 more answers and 51 additional guess-only countries.
- **MVP:** Avoid making tiny or highly obscure island nations frequent or mandatory answers.
- **MVP:** Do not categorically exclude the Caribbean or Oceania; familiar countries may be included.
- **MVP:** Country identifiers and boundaries must be stable and consistent between map, guess input, and distance data.
- **MVP:** Use explicit, separate guess and answer eligibility. All 197 registry countries are guessable immediately with nearest-border feedback. Only 146 have `answerEligible: true`; the remaining 51 never become answers. Separate territories are excluded. Preserve all published targets: the 125-country cycles end on 2032-11-04, the published 126-country cycle runs through 2033-03-10, and the 146-country cycle begins on 2033-03-11. Countries outside the guess registry remain neutral and cannot be submitted. See [the approved list](COUNTRIES.md).

## Later — Discovery and retention
- Recipes with ingredients, steps, and source attribution.
- Digital passport of solved countries and destination cards.
- Saved recipes and favorites.
- Daily streaks, additional statistics, and richer shareable results beyond the MVP emoji share and average guesses per solve.
- Regional challenges, cultural and food trivia, achievements.

## Acceptance tests
1. Two devices in different time zones on the same local calendar date receive the same answer. At the same instant, devices on different local dates receive their respective scheduled answers.
2. Reloading does not change today's answer or erase a player's guesses.
3. A guess displays a finite, nonnegative distance and map proximity feedback.
4. Repeating a guess returns the same feedback and does not increase the guess count.
5. At local midnight, the puzzle changes to the next calendar date's scheduled answer. The countdown accounts for daylight saving changes; UTC midnight alone does not trigger rollover when the local date stays the same.
6. No target repeats within one full eligible-country cycle.
7. Discovery content appears only after solving the puzzle.
8. Mobile and keyboard users can complete the core game.
9. Typing and submitting country names is the guessing mechanism; clicking a map country never selects or submits a guess.
10. Very far guesses use white, very close guesses use red, and white guesses remain distinguishable from unguessed countries. Numeric and text feedback remain usable without color.
11. There is no guess limit or give-up action; basic sharing becomes available only after solving.
12. The country field and submit button are fully visible on initial load, including a 320 × 568 viewport.
13. Map zoom, drag, pinch, reset, and keyboard navigation work without changing the guess count or country input; map gestures do not scroll the whole page.
14. Hovering a map country never shows its name.
15. Detailed boundaries load only when zooming in, remain cached for subsequent zooms, support 32× zoom, and fall back to the usable overview on failure.
16. Entry begins at the top of the map panel and sits directly against the map canvas before the first guess, without a map heading or zoom toolbar. After guessing, a highlighted result notice without a heading appears directly under the submit row and above the map; subsequent guesses compare with the best earlier unrounded distance, including ties, and the notice survives reloads. Completion and the postcard appear together in a modal; a button below the map reopens it after dismissal. Mobile map height is 300–400 px, with sideways panning available at the initial centered 1.2× zoom. The bottom legend and visible instructions are absent; keyboard guidance remains available to screen readers and in “How to play.”
17. History is ordered closest first, including after reload. Correct guesses lead zero-distance ties; other ties retain guess order. Saved progress and share emojis retain the original chronological order.
18. With JavaScript disabled, the initial HTML still advertises the title, description, canonical URL, and a reachable 1200 × 630 PNG preview.
19. Chad and Republic of Chad resolve to TCD, return certified border distances, preserve pre-expansion browser progress, and unlock a sourced postcard when solved in the expanded answer schedule.
20. Solving opens a labeled modal with completion feedback and the postcard. Focus starts on the completion heading and stays inside the modal. Close and Escape restore focus to “View your postcard”; reopening and reloading preserve the win. Long postcards fit and scroll on a 320 × 568 viewport with a reachable close button. Postcard loading failures can retry inside the modal. Local-midnight rollover closes the modal and unlocks the new puzzle.

21. All 197 approved country names resolve as guesses; the 51 guess-only countries have finite distances to every answer and never appear in any schedule. The 20 approved new answers each have a sourced postcard, available only after solving. Preserve all 2,626 previously published assignments, their era metadata and storage identities.
22. New tiny enclaves and special source IDs (Vatican City, San Marino, Monaco, Kosovo, Palestine and South Sudan) resolve to real source boundaries; shared land borders return exactly zero with a separate adjacency flag.
23. Average guesses equals total guesses across valid saved solves divided by solved puzzle count, including the current solve once. Earlier saved completions contribute automatically; unfinished and corrupt progress do not. The postcard and share text agree to one decimal place across reloads, modal reopening, and local-midnight rollover. Storage failures do not prevent completing or sharing the current puzzle.
24. All 146 answer countries have a retained photo with verified bytes, descriptive alt text, an identified author, and a recorded reusable license. Photo requests begin only after solving. The image follows the facts-section heading and precedes its list; attribution remains visible and the rest of the postcard works if the photo fails. Photos fit mobile screens without horizontal overflow.
25. A new distinct guess refocuses immediately without camera animation and fits the computed distance-context envelope with 10% margins when the 1.2× minimum scale permits, including at 320 × 568. The envelope uses at least 250 miles of context; far, polar, and seam-crossing views stop at the initial scale even if cropped. Keyboard, wheel, pinch, automatic framing, reload, and resize never zoom out farther than the initial state. Identical guessed-country/distance/viewport inputs produce identical camera framing regardless of the target. Reload, resize, detail loading, duplicates, invalid input, and local-date reset preserve the approved camera behavior and existing gameplay.
26. All guessable country names and their repeated results fit in a single feedback line at 320 × 568 without horizontal overflow. Long country names may ellipsize; distance, border, comparison, and repeat indicators remain visible. Full country names and descriptive results remain available to screen readers and in history.
